# Problem Discovery

## 1. Problem Context

Product teams receive large volumes of qualitative customer feedback from multiple channels, including:

- Customer support tickets
- NPS and CSAT surveys
- Product reviews
- Customer interviews
- Sales conversations
- Customer Success interactions
- Community forums and other feedback channels

These sources contain valuable signals about customer needs, pain points, feature requests, and overall product experience.

However, customer feedback is often fragmented across different tools, teams, and formats. As feedback volume increases, Product Managers may struggle to systematically identify meaningful patterns and determine which customer problems deserve deeper investigation.

---

## 2. Primary User

The initial target user is a **Product Manager working with moderate-to-large volumes of qualitative customer feedback**.

The Product Manager is responsible for understanding customer problems and translating customer insights into product opportunities, experiments, prioritization decisions, and roadmap discussions.

Potential secondary users include:

- Product Operations
- Product Leadership
- Customer Success
- UX Research

These secondary users require further validation.

---

## 3. Current Workflow

A Product Manager trying to understand customer problems may currently:

1. Review customer support tickets and escalations.
2. Read NPS and CSAT comments.
3. Review product or app-store feedback.
4. Gather feedback from Customer Success and Sales teams.
5. Review user-research findings.
6. Manually identify recurring themes.
7. Compare feedback across customer segments.
8. Estimate the frequency and severity of customer problems.
9. Determine which problems deserve further investigation.
10. Use those findings as one input into product prioritization.

This process often requires information to be manually gathered and synthesized across multiple systems.

---

## 4. Initial Pain-Point Hypotheses

The following represent initial product hypotheses that require validation through research.

### Fragmentation

Customer feedback may exist across multiple systems and teams, making it difficult to develop a unified view of customer problems.

### Volume

As feedback volume increases, manually reviewing every customer signal becomes difficult to scale.

### Pattern Recognition

Recurring problems may be difficult to identify because customers can describe the same underlying problem using different language.

### Recency and Anecdotal Bias

Recent escalations or particularly vocal customers may receive disproportionate attention compared with broader customer patterns.

### Understanding Impact

Frequency alone does not necessarily determine the importance of a customer problem.

Product Managers may also need to understand:

- Problem severity
- Customer segment
- Revenue exposure
- Trend direction
- Strategic importance
- Availability of workarounds

### Traceability and Trust

AI-generated summaries or recommendations may be difficult to trust if Product Managers cannot trace them back to the original customer evidence.

### Prioritization

Customer feedback is an important input into product prioritization, but customer feedback alone should not determine what gets placed on a product roadmap.

---

## 5. Problem Statement

> Product Managers working with large volumes of fragmented qualitative customer feedback need a faster and more systematic way to identify meaningful customer problems, understand their impact, and trace insights back to supporting evidence so they can make better-informed product decisions.

---

## 6. Product Opportunity

There may be an opportunity to create an AI-powered Customer Insights Intelligence platform that helps Product Managers:

- Aggregate fragmented customer feedback
- Identify recurring themes and customer problems
- Detect emerging trends
- Understand affected customer segments
- Connect insights to supporting customer evidence
- Estimate customer and business impact
- Surface potential product opportunities
- Support prioritization and roadmap discussions

The product should function as a **decision-support system rather than autonomously making product decisions**.

---

## 7. Initial Product Hypothesis

> If Product Managers can use AI to identify recurring customer problems across fragmented feedback while retaining access to the underlying customer evidence and relevant business context, they can move from customer signals to actionable product opportunities faster and with greater confidence.

---

## 8. Key Assumptions to Validate

Before defining the final product and MVP, the following assumptions require validation:

1. Product Managers spend meaningful time manually synthesizing qualitative customer feedback.
2. Feedback fragmentation creates a significant challenge for product teams.
3. Product Managers have difficulty identifying patterns across large volumes of qualitative feedback.
4. Existing tools do not completely address the transition from customer feedback to product opportunity.
5. Product Managers are more likely to trust AI-generated insights when supporting customer evidence is available.
6. Business context improves the usefulness of customer-feedback analysis.
7. Product Managers prefer AI-assisted prioritization over autonomous roadmap decisions.

These assumptions will guide subsequent product discovery, MVP definition, and validation.
